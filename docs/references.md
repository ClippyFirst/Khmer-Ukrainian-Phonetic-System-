# References

## Core linguistic sources

1. Unicode Consortium. *Unicode Standard 17.0.0*, Chapter 16: South and Southeast Asian Scripts — Khmer. Script and encoding structure.
2. Ishida, Richard (2026). *Khmer (Cambodian) Orthography Notes*. Modern orthography, consonant classes, vowel behaviour and descriptive phonology.
3. Chem, Vatho (2020). The Standard Khmer vowel system: An acoustic study. *Insight: Cambodia Journal of Basic and Applied Research*, 2(2), 57–88. DOI: 10.61945/cjbar.2020.2.2.3. The publisher metadata repeats the same author for two affiliations; it does not identify two distinct authors.
4. Henderson, E. J. A. (1952). The Main features of Cambodian Pronunciation. *Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies*, 14(1), 149–174.
5. Wayland, Ratree P., & Guion, Susan G. (2005). Sound changes following the loss of /r/ in Khmer: a new tonogenetic mechanism? *Mon-Khmer Studies*, 35, 55–82. DOI: 10.15144/MKSJ-35.55.
6. Kirby, James P. (2014). Incipient tonogenesis in Phnom Penh Khmer: Acoustic and perceptual studies. *Journal of Phonetics*, 43, 69–85. DOI: 10.1016/j.wocn.2014.02.001.
7. Kirby, James P. (2014). Incipient tonogenesis in Phnom Penh Khmer: Computational studies. *Laboratory Phonology*, 5(1), 195–230. DOI: 10.1515/lp-2014-0008.

## Audio and supplementary research data

8. Kirby, James (2015). *Incipient tonogenesis in Phnom Penh Khmer: audio recordings, 2011* [sound]. University of Edinburgh, School of Philosophy, Psychology and Language Sciences, Linguistics and English Language. DOI: 10.7488/ds/321. The DataShare item metadata declares CC BY 4.0; the archive contains 24-bit, 44.1 kHz recordings of 16 Phnom Penh speakers and Praat TextGrids.
9. Kirby, James (2015). *Incipient tonogenesis in Phnom Penh Khmer: supplementary materials, 2011* [dataset]. University of Edinburgh, School of Philosophy, Psychology and Language Sciences, Linguistics and English Language. DOI: 10.7488/ds/319. The DataShare item metadata declares CC BY 4.0; includes anonymised production/perception data and analysis materials.

## Variety and dialect evidence

10. Woźnica, Piotr (2013). *Procesy fonetyczno-fonologiczne w nieregionalnej odmianie języka khmerskiego* [doctoral thesis; English title: *Phonetic-phonological processes in non-regional Khmer*]. Adam Mickiewicz University. Repository record: https://repozytorium.amu.edu.pl/items/07609606-333b-4603-95f3-3cb14c1eb083. The thesis analyses acoustic data from 80 speakers across Cambodia; its sampling limitations must accompany any claim about a country-wide standard.

12. Oller, D. Kimbrough, & Ramsdell, Heather L. (2006). A weighted reliability measure for phonetic transcription. *Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research*, 49(6), 1391–1411. DOI: 10.1044/1092-4388(2006/100).

## External G2P benchmarks and comparators

13. Shurtz, Ammon, Richardson, Christian, & Richardson, Stephen D. (2026). MekongPhon: A Large-Scale Parallel IPA Corpus for Lao and Khmer. *Proceedings of LREC 2026*, 1650–1658. DOI: 10.63317/4bb9rvdshu4z. The paper/proceedings licence does not establish separate rights to the corpus, source dictionaries or derived data.
14. Sar, Vathnak, & Tan, Tien-Ping (2019). Applying Linguistic G2P Knowledge on a Statistical Grapheme-to-phoneme Conversion in Khmer. *Procedia Computer Science*, 161, 415–423. DOI: 10.1016/j.procs.2019.11.140. The article's internal training/test-count and baseline-WER discrepancies are recorded in the external benchmark audit.

## Comparative transcription sources

15. UNGEGN. *Transliteration of Khmer Writing* — comparative geographic-name romanization reference.
16. *Khmer-Russian practical transcription* — secondary web reproduction citing Gorgoniyev, Serdyuchenko and the 1967 Cambodian geographic-name instructions. It is retained as comparative evidence, not as a primary verified standard.

It is not cited as a peer-reviewed 2026 paper; the full dictionary is absent from the inspected tree and its README/dictionary licence statements conflict.
